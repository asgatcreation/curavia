One patient, one record

The handover is the product.

Most hospital software is a filing cabinet with a login. The expensive problem in a real hospital is not storing the record — it is the half hour a patient spends walking a paper slip from one department to the next, and the prescription that reaches the pharmacy after the patient does.

  1. Front deskRegistered, token issued
  2. TriageVitals, early warning score
  3. DoctorNotes, diagnosis, orders
  4. LaboratorySample, result, critical flag
  5. PharmacyChecked against allergies
  6. CashierBill, payer, receipt
Nothing is typed twice. The triage nurse's vitals are already on the doctor's screen. The doctor's prescription is already in the pharmacy queue, with the patient's allergies checked and the stock position known. The bill is already at the cashier, priced for that patient's HMO. The journey board shows where every patient in the building is standing right now.

Built here, not translated

It already knows how a Nigerian hospital works.

₦

Money in naira, stored as whole kobo. Never a floating-point number, so nothing is ever lost to rounding across a day of bills.

✓

HMO, NHIA and corporate schemes. Enrolee checks, tariffs per payer, pre-authorisation codes and claim batches — not a single "insurance" field.

✓

NAFDAC numbers, batches and expiry dates on every medicine, with stock held as a ledger so a count can always be explained.

✓

The national immunisation schedule in the child-health module, and antenatal care that follows the way clinics actually book it.

✓

West Africa Time everywhere, and phone numbers in the format your patients actually give you.

✓

Verified before going live: CAC registration, a state Ministry of Health facility licence, and the medical director's MDCN number.

Privacy by construction

A hospital's records are its own.

No hospital can reach another's data

Enforced at the database layer, not remembered by each developer. A query without a hospital in scope raises an error rather than returning everything — and a test suite deliberately attacks the boundary on every push.

Every view is audited, not just every change

Opening a record is itself recorded, in an append-only log chained with SHA-256 so an altered or deleted entry can be detected. A hospital can prove who looked at a file.

Curavia's own staff cannot read your records

Not as a policy — as a permission. Support access needs a time-limited grant the hospital issues, and everything done under it is written to the hospital's own audit log.
